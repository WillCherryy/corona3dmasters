### A13. What the H-CO2 data files contain (checked 6 Nov 2026 on the 5 eV file, `E5.00-DCS.txt`)
- Each source file holds 27 blocks of 1,800 rows (angle 0.1 to 180 degrees, differential cross section in Angstrom^2 per steradian). The header line of each block gives the initial and final CO2 rotational state: `LEV(I),J(I)` = 1, 0 (CO2 starts in j = 0 in every block) and `LEV(F),J(F)` = the final state.
- Block 1 (`LEV(F),J(F) = 1 0`, final j' = 0): ELASTIC. CO2 stays in its starting rotational state; no energy goes into rotation.
- Blocks 2 to 27 (final j' = 2, 4, ..., 52): ROTATIONALLY INELASTIC. The H atom spins CO2 up and loses energy; only even j' occur (symmetry of CO2). These are generally deflected through larger angles.
- User's `test` branch files = block 1 only, converted Angstrom^2 -> cm^2 (x 1e-16). Verified row by row against the source: 1,800 rows, identical angles, maximum relative difference 2e-16.
- Integrated cross sections at 5 eV: elastic (j' = 0) 2.21e-15 cm^2; j' = 2: 5.3e-16; j' = 10: 1.2e-16; j' = 20: 7.8e-17; j' = 52: 1.5e-17; sum over all 27 blocks 4.58e-15. Elastic is therefore about 48% of all collisions at 5 eV.
- Angular character (own trapezoid integration of the tables; mean of 1 - cos(theta), proportional to momentum transfer): O-H (Zhang) 0.33 at 1 eV and 0.20 at 5 eV; H-CO2 elastic only 0.011 and 0.006; H-CO2 total (all blocks) 0.23 and 0.09. The elastic-only scattering is almost entirely straight-ahead, so energy lost per collision with CO2 falls from about 1.5% (O-H angles with CO2 mass) to under 0.1% at 1 eV.
- DECISION (user, 6 Nov 2026): run elastic-only H-CO2 now, because the existing O-H, N2 and CO data are also elastic (Zhang et al. 2009). Integrating the inelastic channels (sum over blocks, the "total" variant, files already available on branch `claude/exciting-keller-mu4f4t`) is a planned next step after the other results are verified. In the thesis, describe the result as the elastic-channel approximation and give the 48% figure; note that the elastic-only escape rate is expected to be an upper bound for CO2.

- QUOTE (Gregory et al. 2023, as supplied by the user): "We neglect inelastic collisions as they are unimportant relative to elastic collisions at the low energies relevant to H scattering through an atmosphere predominantly composed of much heavier species (Lichtenegger et al., 2006)".
- CONSEQUENCE: running the elastic-only H-CO2 data is consistent with the paper's stated treatment (all partners elastic). The planned inelastic step is therefore a direct test of the paper's assumption for the CO2 partner.
- TWO READINGS OF "NEGLECT INELASTIC COLLISIONS" for H + CO2 (to be settled against the paper and Lichtenegger et al. 2006): (a) neglect the ENERGY lost to rotation but keep the scattering angles of those collisions (use the summed-over-final-states DCS with the model's elastic kinematics: this is the "total" variant); (b) delete the inelastic collisions altogether (the elastic-channel DCS and sigma only: this is the elastic-only variant now running). The two differ strongly for CO2: at 5 eV the rotationally inelastic cross sections add up to about 52% of the total, and the mean (1 - cos theta) is 0.23 (summed) against 0.011 (elastic channel alone) at 1 eV, giving about 2.3 times the HSA escape rate.
- ENERGY SCALE OF THE ROTATIONAL TRANSITIONS (own estimate, B(CO2) about 0.39 cm^-1, E = B j'(j'+1)): j' = 2: 0.3 meV; j' = 10: 5 meV; j' = 52: 0.13 eV. At 5 eV the maximum recoil loss to a CO2 target is about 0.44 eV (8.8% of 5 eV), so energy given to rotation is usually small compared with it except for the highest j'. This supports "energy loss to rotation is minor" but does not by itself justify removing the collisions' angular deflection.

### A25. What Gregory et al. (2023) say about collisions (read from the PDF in the uploads, 8 Nov 2026) and consequences
- COLLISION TREATMENT (Section 2, quoted): the model tracks test particles that "collide elastically with neutral background species (CO, CO2, N2, and O). We use the total and differential O-H cross sections of Zhang et al. (2009), without modification, for collisions with all four background species due to a lack of availability of more specific data." The collision test is exp(-sum sigma_s n_s v dt); the scattering angle is sampled from the differential cross section "for the energy closest to the computed energy (from 0.1, 0.5, 1, or 5 eV)"; "We neglect inelastic collisions as they are unimportant relative to elastic collisions at the low energies relevant to H scattering through an atmosphere predominantly composed of much heavier species (Lichtenegger et al., 2006)."
- LIMITATIONS SECTION (quoted): "this model only considers elastic collisions with limited background species. The lack of inelastic collisions may result in some overestimation of particle energies and therefore escape. However, inelastic channels are insignificant for atom-atom collisions at these energies (Lewkow & Kharchenko, 2014), and we follow the assumptions of similar hot H Monte Carlo modeling, which suggests that inelastic atom-molecule collisions are also unimportant at energies below 8 eV (Lammer et al., 2006; Lichtenegger et al., 2006). ... we opt instead to use unscaled O-H cross sections for collisions of H with all background species until dedicated cross sections for each collision pair are generated ... This assumption likely affects our results the most, and we will update our model to use the relevant cross sections as they become available." Also: no H-H or H-H2 collisions are modelled (see B10).
- CONSEQUENCES: (1) the O-H data for all four partners is the authors' stated baseline, so our baselines are consistent with the paper; (2) the H-CO2 work is exactly the update the authors say they intend; (3) in the paper "elastic" means the model's elastic kinematics with O-H data (atom-atom, where total = elastic); for H + CO2 the sigma and the DCS must describe the same set of outcomes: elastic sigma with the j' = 0 DCS (current runs), or total sigma with the sum over all final states (total variant); mixing total sigma with the elastic DCS would count inelastic collisions but scatter them forward as if elastic, which is inconsistent; (4) Bop and Gacesa show the rotationally inelastic channels are about half of all collisions at 5 eV for H + CO2, so the paper's premise ("inelastic unimportant") can be tested for CO2.
- DISCREPANCY TO CHECK: the paper lists DCS energies 0.1, 0.5, 1 and 5 eV; the repository's species configs and O-H files use 0.1, 1, 5 and 10 eV (no 0.5 eV file in `inputs/collisions/O-H`). The baselines used the repository's files and reproduce the paper's totals to about 1%, so any effect looks small, but it should be confirmed with the authors or the data release.
- D-O DATA LEAD: the reference list gives the source as Zhang, Kharchenko, Jamieson and Dalgarno (2009), "Energy relaxation in collisions of hydrogen and deuterium with oxygen atoms", J. Geophys. Res. 114, A07101, doi:10.1029/2009JA014055. The title indicates that the same paper treats deuterium + oxygen; its tables or supplement should be checked first for the O-D total and differential cross sections (format and energies to be verified).
